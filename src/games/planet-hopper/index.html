Planets and comets use reference elliptical orbits, including orbital inclinations and retrograde motion. Planets, dwarf planets and moons are enlarged about 92× relative to heliocentric distances; the Sun remains enlarged about 31×. Moon distances are also enlarged 92×, preserving each system’s size/distance proportions. Moon planes use ecliptic, equatorial or local Laplace references; their fixed orientations are approximate. Planetary and moon animation clocks are accelerated separately, and starting phases are illustrative, not live positions. Asteroid-belt rocks, asteroid/comet sizes and the ship remain illustrative. Flight and the camera adapt for close-up visits.

Orbit references: JPL planetary elements, satellite mean elements, Small-Body Database and IAU pole orientations. Elements are fixed snapshots at mixed reference epochs, not an ephemeris.

The right-side information panel includes additional facts, Wikipedia articles and credited Wikimedia Commons reference images. Scroll the panel to see all details. Some images are illustrations or low-resolution spacecraft views. Choose Load reference image to connect to Wikimedia Commons; this shares connection information such as your IP address. Descriptions and surface textures are included locally in the game.

Earth, Mercury, Mars, Jupiter and the Moon use locally stored surface maps. Earth: NASA Blue Marble, Reto Stöckli, Robert Simmon and MODIS teams (source). Mercury: MESSENGER, Johns Hopkins APL and USGS (source; grayscale). Mars: NASA/JPL/USGS Viking mosaic (source; colorized). Jupiter: NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute Cassini (source). Moon: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, Ernie Wright, LRO/LROC (source). Some maps are resized for the game; other surfaces remain illustrative.

After autopilot arrives, dragging changes the view while the spacecraft follows its destination. Flight keys or touch controls release it. Dragging during an autopilot journey cancels the journey.
